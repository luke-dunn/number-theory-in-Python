# Suggested Number Theory Explorations  
*Based on current script set – April 16, 2025*

## 📐 Primes & Sequences
- **Chen primes**  
  Primes \( p \) such that \( p + 2 \) is either a prime or a semiprime.

- **Proth primes**  
  Numbers of the form \( k \cdot 2^n + 1 \) with \( k < 2^n \), which may be prime.

- **Lehmer's totient problem**  
  Investigate whether a composite number \( n \) can satisfy \( \phi(n) \mid (n-1) \).

- **Primorial primes**  
  Primes of the form \( p\# \pm 1 \), where \( p\# \) is the product of all primes ≤ \( p \).

## 🧮 Sums, Products, and Representations
- **Waring's problem**  
  Explore representations of numbers as sums of \( k \)th powers (e.g., 4 = 2², 3 = 1² + 1² + 1²).

- **Egyptian fractions**  
  Represent rational numbers as sums of distinct unit fractions.

- **Partition function**  
  Study the number of ways to write an integer as a sum of positive integers.

- **Riesel numbers**  
  Odd numbers \( k \) such that \( k \cdot 2^n - 1 \) is always composite.

## 🔢 Function & Series Explorations
- **Liouville function \( \lambda(n) \)**  
  Returns \( (-1)^{\Omega(n)} \), indicating the parity of the total number of prime factors.

- **Moebius function \( \mu(n) \)**  
  Useful in inversion formulas and multiplicative function analysis.

- **Dedekind eta function**  
  A modular form with deep connections to partitions and q-series.

- **Dirichlet convolution**  
  Operation on arithmetic functions; useful for exploring multiplicative structures.

## 💠 Properties & Patterns
- **Highly composite numbers**  
  Numbers with more divisors than any smaller integer.

- **Amicable and sociable numbers**  
  Pairs or cycles of numbers where each is the sum of the proper divisors of the next.

- **Digital roots and persistence**  
  Explore additive and multiplicative persistence of integers.

- **Niven (Harshad) numbers**  
  Integers divisible by the sum of their digits.

## 🔍 Conjectures & Open Problems
- **Legendre’s conjecture**  
  There is always a prime between \( n^2 \) and \( (n+1)^2 \).

- **Brocard’s problem**  
  Find \( n, m \) such that \( n! + 1 = m^2 \); only three solutions known.

- **Erdős–Straus conjecture**  
  Every \( 4/n \) is the sum of three distinct unit fractions for \( n \geq 2 \).

- **Bunyakovsky conjecture**  
  Conditions under which a polynomial with integer coefficients produces infinitely many primes.
